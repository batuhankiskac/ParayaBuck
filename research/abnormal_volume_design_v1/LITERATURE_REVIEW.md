# Literature review

## Primary foundation

Gervais, Kaniel, and Mingelgrin (2001), “The High-Volume Return Premium,” *Journal of Finance* 56, 877–919 ([paper](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00349)) studies NYSE stocks from August 1963 through December 1996. Its main daily object is stock-specific dollar volume, approximated by last price times shares traded. Formation-day dollar volume is ranked with the preceding 49 trading days. Ranks 46–50 are high volume and ranks 1–5 are low volume. This is an own-history ordinal shock, not a cross-sectional level, z-score, or turnover measure.

The daily high leg outperformed same-size controls while the low leg underperformed them; the two legs were of similar magnitude. The weekly spread was more dependent on the low leg. The daily construction therefore supplies the stronger long-only precedent. A prespecified robustness/control retains formation returns in ranks 16–35 of the stock's own 50-return history; it strengthened the result and better isolates volume from same-day price pressure. Main holding periods were 1, 10, or 20 trading days without interim rebalancing, and main test intervals did not overlap.

GKM found the gross effect across size groups but weaker in large stocks. Market-order execution at quoted prices was unattractive. A limit-order simulation was only mildly profitable for small/medium stocks, improved by the middle-40% return control, and was not positive for large stocks; brokerage and market impact were incomplete. Modern net profitability is therefore an open gate, not a literature conclusion.

## Important boundary: turnover is not the signal

Lee and Swaminathan (2000), “Price Momentum and Trading Volume,” *Journal of Finance* 55, 2017–2069 ([paper](https://www.lsvasset.com/pdf/research-papers/Price-Momentum-Trad-Vol-2000.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00280)) uses average daily turnover—shares traded divided by point-in-time shares outstanding—over 3/6/9/12-month momentum windows. It is a persistent volume-level and momentum interaction, not GKM's 49-day shock. The repository lacks the required point-in-time shares-outstanding history. No turnover proxy or hybrid is admitted.

## Later evidence

- Kaniel, Ozoguz, and Starks (2012), *Journal of Financial Economics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.08.012)), applies an own-stock 49-day share-volume reference in 41 countries. Evidence is mostly positive in developed markets and mixed in emerging markets; effects weaken with size and index membership. Institutional execution costs erase the reported spread, while some retail-cost cases survive.
- Wang (2021), *Journal of Financial Economics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.10.006)), studies U.S. common stocks through 2016 using month-end formation volume relative to the preceding 49 days and a monthly hold. Factor, business-cycle/macro, and mispricing explanations do not fully account for the high-minus-low premium. The 1963–2016 sample provides post-publication-period coverage; that coverage is a subperiod robustness fact, not a control. The paper does not establish that a concentrated high leg alone is net profitable.
- Israeli, Kaniel, and Sridharan, “Real Side of the High-Volume Return Premium,” *Management Science* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3886)), links abnormal volume to later financing and investment behavior. This supports a visibility/attention channel but is not a return replication.
- Evidence is not universal. Wang, Wen, and Singh (2017) find no overall HVRP in Chinese equities and a conditional high-volume discount concentrated in speculative stocks and the recent subperiod, especially among small firms and firms with low institutional ownership or analyst coverage ([Pacific-Basin Finance Journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927538X16302773)). Other country studies likewise show size, investor-type, state, and horizon dependence.

## Literature gate

`CONDITIONAL_PASS`. The literature supports one frozen daily own-history abnormal-dollar-volume experiment. It does not establish modern large/liquid, concentrated, long-only, after-cost alpha. That exact proposition must pass the future gates without redesign.
