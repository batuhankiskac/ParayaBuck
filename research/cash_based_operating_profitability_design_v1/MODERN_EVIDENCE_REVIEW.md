# Modern evidence review

## Verdict

`MODERN_CBOP_LONG_LEG_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`

The original 1963–2014 evidence is economically interesting, and independent
replications preserve a diversified long-short cash-profitability effect. That
does not establish a modern concentrated long-only strategy.

Hou, Xue and Zhang's replication reports positive value-weighted long-short
spreads for both a current-assets-deflated variant and the lagged-assets
variant closest to BGLN. The q-factor alphas also remain positive. This is
diversified spread evidence, not top-six evidence. [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)

Fama and French (2018) prefer a diversified factor model in which cash
profitability replaces ordinary operating profitability. Again, this supports
a factor, not a six-stock or one-stock implementation.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.02.012)

Welch and Chen's 2026 working paper is the most direct modern caution. In the
post-2005 intersection of the top 3,000 stocks and top 90% of market
capitalization, Table 2 reports CbOP raw long-short 0.66% monthly and raw
long-minus-market 0.40%, but empirical-Bayes-adjusted results fall to 0.06%
and 0.00%, respectively. Their design requires at least 20 names per leg.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06502)

The exact CbOP long leg must not inherit evidence from generic quality, gross
profitability, EBITDA, or ordinary OP factors. Nor may positive high-minus-low
evidence be presented as proof that the high leg alone survives modern
selection correction and costs.

## Judgments

- `MODERN_US_LARGE_LIQUID_LONG_SHORT_EVIDENCE_PRESENT`
- `MODERN_CBOP_LONG_LEG_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`
- no published top-six or top-one CbOP test was found;
- transaction-cost and capacity intuition is favorable for annual diversified
  rebalancing, but cannot rescue unsupported concentration.
