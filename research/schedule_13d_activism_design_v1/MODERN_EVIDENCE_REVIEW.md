# Modern evidence review

## Published-after-2015 evidence

- von Lilienfeld-Toal and Schnitzler (2020), [Journal of Corporate Finance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S092911992030064X), study initial 13Ds from 2001–2016. Their 40-day filing-centered window combines pre-filing, disclosure, and post-filing days; small targets are heavily represented. It is not a next-open result.
- Swanson, Young, and Yust (2022), [Journal of Corporate Finance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0929119921002662), use 1994–2014. They find an announcement effect and no average long-run reversal, with weaker results among the largest targets. This does not isolate positive +1-onward alpha.
- Kedia, Starks, and Wang (2021), [Review of Corporate Finance Studies](https://doi.org/10.1093/rcfs/cfaa027), find higher long-run returns conditional on pre-existing activism-friendly institutional ownership. That is a conditional cross-sectional result, not an unconditional public 13D long leg.
- The SEC final rule reviews the activism literature and sample periods at pp. 228–229: <https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf>.

## Temporal relevance

Recent publication dates do not make the underlying campaigns modern. The principal samples end in 2010, 2014, or 2016; they do not test a 2017–2026 acceptance-timestamp next-open strategy, and none evaluates the post-2024 five-business-day regime as a trading rule.

Market dissemination is faster, and the 2024 rule shortens the undisclosed interval. Either change could alter the split between pre-filing and filing-time price discovery. Directional intuition is not a substitute for a modern implementable portfolio result.

## Verdict

`MODERN_POST_FILING_LONG_ONLY_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`

Modern work confirms that activism can matter and that announcement gains need not reverse. It does not prove a robust unconditional outsider alpha after public dissemination.
