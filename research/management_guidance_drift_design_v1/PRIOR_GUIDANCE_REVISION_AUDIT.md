# Prior-management-guidance revisions

Verdict: `PRIOR_GUIDANCE_REVISION_IS_INDEPENDENTLY_SUPPORTED`, limited to independent practitioner research and related non-US academic evidence. It is NOT a valid substitute for the Ng, Tuna and Verdi management-minus-analyst-consensus surprise, and no candidate switch is authorized.

## Evidence found, with limits

S&P Capital IQ's January 2016 research explicitly examines guidance higher than previous guidance as well as guidance above analyst consensus, using US observations through February 2015. The official research summary reports positive excess returns for these positive-news events. That is direct independent empirical motivation for revisions, so it would be inaccurate to say no supporting research exists. It does not establish exact equivalence to consensus surprise, an isolated modern revision long leg after costs, or a scarce-slot rule. [Primary research summary](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/what-does-earnings-guidance-tell-us); [primary report](https://www.spglobal.com/content/dam/spglobal/mi/en/documents/general/what-does-earnings-guidance-tell-us-listen-when-management-announces-good-news.pdf).

Nakagawa and Yamanishi (2014), *Relations Between Management Earnings Forecast Revisions and Returns*, reports revision-related hedge returns under Japanese disclosure practice. Its abstract includes months BEFORE revision release within the measured year. This is neither a clean post-public US long leg nor replication of the requested signal. [Original university publication](https://ousar.lib.okayama-u.ac.jp/53098).

Zuo (2016), *The informational feedback effect of stock prices on management forecasts*, studies US management forecast revisions during 1996–2010 and links them to contemporaneous stock-price information. This addresses managers learning from prices, not necessarily future returns earned after a revision. A price/revision correlation must not be presented as evidence of delayed tradable alpha. [Final publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2016.03.001).

## Why substitution changes the hypothesis

For the same EPS basis, share units and fiscal period, a new forecast can exceed old management guidance while falling below current analyst expectations. Conversely, it can be below old guidance yet above a recently reduced consensus. The signals can therefore have opposite signs. This is a logical identification distinction; no local examples or economic test were calculated.

A prior-guidance revision requires its own public previous-forecast history, unchanged accounting basis, identical fiscal period, range convention and revision-status ledger. Reaffirmation, withdrawal, new-period initial guidance and split/GAAP changes cannot be treated as numeric revisions by default. Historical management statements do not recreate analysts' contemporaneous beliefs.

The revision idea is related voluntary-disclosure research but a materially different hypothesis requiring a separate future pre-outcome design and evidence review. The present campaign retains the literature-faithful management-versus-consensus question and its licensed-data dependency. No revision score, threshold, holding period or alternative candidate is frozen here.
