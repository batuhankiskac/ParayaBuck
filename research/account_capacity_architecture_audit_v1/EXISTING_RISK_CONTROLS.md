## What the count cap uniquely adds

The implemented cap bounds the number of distinct `(market, canonical symbol)` account slots allowed in a decision, including fixed external inventory and working-order claims (`execution/portfolio.py:267–308`). Gross, sleeve, and per-name controls are weight constraints; they do not bound the number of arbitrarily small positive target lines. The count cap can therefore limit **cardinality** and the possible number of order/monitoring lines. Its exact threshold of six has no documented independent capacity or risk derivation in the source/history reviewed here. It is also a diversification ceiling: with two crypto slots, only four equity slots remain, independently of the 0.55 equity sleeve and 0.10 equity per-name limits. This is static geometry, not a claim about realized holdings.

## Limits of the inventory

Scanner controls are upstream eligibility gates and depend on the selected active-universe snapshot; they are not per-order live liquidity checks. Target gross/cash/sleeve/per-name checks occur before execution; the fresh BUY phase explicitly rechecks cash and gross, but does not itself replay every target-level filter. Failures, stale data, and external/manual inventory can still leave targets underdeployed. No historical positions, orders, returns, or candidate economics were read for this document.
