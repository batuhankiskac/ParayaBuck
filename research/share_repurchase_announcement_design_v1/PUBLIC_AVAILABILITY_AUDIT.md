# Public availability audit

No sample row has a verified earliest public instant. Two rows have exact SEC Accepted fields. Distinguish `board_approved_at`, `release_dated`, `sec_accepted_at`, `publicly_disseminated_at`, `observed_at`, and `earliest_verified_public_at`.

[Apple's index](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019324000067/0000320193-24-000067-index.htm) reports 2024-05-02 16:30:34; [Microsoft's](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312516742796/0001193125-16-742796-index.html) reports 2016-10-20 16:10:25. Preserve the source text and normalize verified Eastern time with `America/New_York`, including daylight saving. Filing date and report period are not substitutes for an instant.

The [SEC access guide](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) warns that some submissions starting after 17:30 ET disseminate the next business day. Consequently, acceptance must be paired with dissemination evidence or a conservative verified availability bound. Missing/ambiguous timezone or late-filing dissemination is a fail-closed condition; never assume immediate public delivery for every accepted submission. Current index corrections also require immutable local capture and a correction ledger.

An earlier issuer release may make SEC acceptance a conservative later availability reference, not the first announcement. Microsoft has a [dated September release](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2016/09/20/microsoft-announces-quarterly-dividend-increase-and-share-repurchase-program-2/) and a subsequent October filing. The current issuer page supports a historical date claim; without archived timestamp provenance it does not prove an exact tradable release time. Reusing October acceptance as a fresh event would falsely restart its information clock. A delayed-SEC subset needs its own external thesis; it cannot automatically inherit evidence about first public announcements.

For an eventually justified candidate, execute at the first regular-session open strictly after validated availability and required ingestion readiness, with exchange calendar/holiday handling. If dissemination timing could cross an open, exclude until resolved. Never assign midnight, use undisclosed board dates, capture announcement-close prices retroactively, or optimize intraday timing. A date-only non-SEC release is insufficient for a first-open claim. A safely later open can avoid lookahead but changes the event lag and requires prior design justification.

Apple's exact after-close acceptance illustrates daily scheduling compatibility, not profitable persistence to the following open. Lands’ End and Bank of America lack retrieved acceptance evidence in this sample. No execution dates or market observations were calculated. The sample establishes neither historical timestamp distribution nor delay tolerance.
