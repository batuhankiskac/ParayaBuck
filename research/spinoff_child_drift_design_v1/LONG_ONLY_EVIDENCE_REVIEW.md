# Positive child long-leg audit

Retrieved 2026-09-22. The proposed asset is the **newly traded child common share**. Parent, pro-forma combined, announcement, ETF, and spread returns are excluded.

| Original primary evidence | Positive child leg | Timing and qualification |
|---|---|---|
| [Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge (1993), full text Table 3](https://longrunplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/restructuring-through-spinoffs.pdf) | At 6/12/24/36 months, child mean raw buy-and-hold: 7.7/19.9/52.0/76.0%; size/industry matched excess: −1.0/4.5/25.0/33.6%. | 146 1965–88 children, buy at first trading-day **close**. 24/36-month abnormal effect is statistically significant; 6/12 is not. 89 first traded OTC. Later takeovers account for much of 2–3-year excess. |

| [McConnell, Sibley and Xu (2015), author-hosted full text](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf) | 153 children from 2001–12: 22-month mean raw +31.7%, size/BM benchmark +14.6%, child difference +17.1%; 36-month raw +49.4% versus benchmark +22.8%. | Return compounding begins **after** ex-date, an outsider-relevant clock in principle. It is a diversified equal-child result, right-skewed and not a next-open execution test. |

There is credible historical positive **child** ownership evidence, especially for a broad all-child portfolio. Its strength changes sharply with sample, benchmark, horizon, and rare large winners. No table above establishes the positive expected **selected top-six** leg, positive **top-one** leg, net transaction-cost result, or a post-2015 child cohort. The classic 33.6% number is a 36-month *matched-firm-adjusted mean for 146 tax-free pure children bought at the first day close*, not an annualized return or next-open result.
