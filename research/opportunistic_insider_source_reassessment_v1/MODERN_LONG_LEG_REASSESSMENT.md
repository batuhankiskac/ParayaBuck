# Modern liquid long-leg reassessment

## Verdict

`MODERN_LIQUID_LONG_LEG_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`

Cohen, Malloy, and Pomorski's original sample supports the taxonomy, but not
the exact modern mandate. Their Table IV Panel B reports value-weighted
opportunistic-buy five-factor alpha of about 0.72% monthly (t=2.27). The often
cited 0.82% (t=2.15) figure is the opportunistic buy-minus-sell portfolio, not
a buy-only result. Appendix A11's large-stock opportunistic-buy coefficient is
about 0.55% (t=2.31). The sample ends in 2007.

Primary paper:
[Cohen, Malloy, and Pomorski PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037e-2b77-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content).

Later evidence does not establish persistence for a liquid, scalable,
public-filing copy strategy:

- Gangopadhyay and Yook report materially lower opportunistic insider profits
  after Dodd-Frank ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/JFRC-02-2021-0018)).
- Oenschläger and Möllenhoff study 25,636 public Form 4 buy signals from
  November 2018 to November 2023. In the upper-liquidity quartile the immediate
  percentage return is small, turns negative by day five, dollar returns are
  insignificant, and liquidity loading is adverse through day ten—before
  trading costs ([manuscript](https://elekpub.bib.uni-wuppertal.de/ubwhs/download/pdf/7754587),
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2024.106514)).

This is a literature verdict only. No ParayaBuck returns, price bars, factor
regressions, or V3 correlations were computed.
