# Primary literature review

He and Narayanamoorthy, *Earnings acceleration and stock returns*, Journal of Accounting and Economics 69(1), 101238 (2020), DOI [10.1016/j.jacceco.2019.101238](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2019.101238), is the published authority. The accessible [March 2018 author draft](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/2018_00M_Earnings-Acceleration-and-Stock-Returns_NAAIM-Submission-with-title-page.pdf), sections 2.1-2.2, Tables 1-3, supplies the detailed equations and tables. The published abstract confirms the 1972-2015 sample, top/bottom deciles, two post-announcement windows, and price/small-cap robustness. Draft and published descriptions agree on the mechanism; precise implementation below is attributed to the draft where publisher full text was inaccessible.

The hypothesis is a second difference of seasonally differenced quarterly EPS, scaled by prior-quarter price. The proposed mechanism is underreaction to implications for earnings growth two and three fiscal quarters later (draft section 3), distinct from a one-quarter seasonal surprise. The paper studies diversified deciles; its headline 1.8% one-month and 3.4% quarter-long market-adjusted figures are *long-short spreads*, not concentrated long-only returns. No local future outcome was read or calculated.

The paper's primary EAP is price deflated; EAA (absolute historical EPS deflator), sales acceleration, profitability acceleration, specific growth patterns, profitability, and volatility filters are variants and are outside V1. The draft bases decile cutoffs on the previous fiscal quarter's acceleration distribution and scales rank 0..9 to -0.5..+0.5 for regressions (section 2.1). Its day 2 to day 30 and day 2 to next-announcement-plus-one windows need a trading-calendar/open-close clarification before executable freezing.
