# Focused literature review

This review is evidence for one later design, not a list of strategies to
screen. No local ParayaBuck outcome was used. The relevant distinction is
between a stock's regular-session close-to-open price component and its
open-to-close component; the papers below often use richer CRSP/TAQ data than
the local OHLC package.

| Lou, Polk & Skouras, *A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns* (2019, JFE 134) ([author version](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf), [journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19300650)) | U.S. CRSP/Compustat stocks, 1993–2013; characteristic portfolios. | Decomposes characteristic-strategy returns into overnight and intraday components and tests persistence across horizons up to 60 months. | Close-to-open versus open-to-close; robustness includes the first half-hour VWAP. The proposed explanation is a persistent tug of war between overnight and daytime investor clienteles. Same-period continuation and cross-period reversal can coexist. | It is not a single OHLC stock-ranking strategy and its characteristic portfolios are not the current ParayaBuck universe. It is strong evidence that the cross-period sign is not interchangeable with conventional close-to-close momentum, but it does not license copying a published parameter. |

The mechanism is period-specific price formation/risk, not a direct trading signal. | Opening-price definition materially matters, and the paper is not a long-only overnight-shock strategy. Mechanism: attention-driven retail demand creates temporary opening price pressure. | Large/liquid coverage is directly relevant, but the attention proxy and pre-2010 microstructure are dated. The paper supports a contrarian direction but does not establish the exact one-session, SPY-excess implementation below. |

Mechanisms include information accumulation during closure and opening microstructure. | Older data and aggregate/index emphasis make exact current-stock applicability weaker. ## Reversal versus continuation

The decision is **REVERSAL** for the relationship of an overnight move to the
following regular-session price-formation interval.

The evidence is not that every overnight move reverses. Continuation appears in
same-period and longer-horizon components, and overnight returns can contain
information. The narrower cross-period question has a more coherent U.S. stock
mechanism: overnight clientele/attention and opening liquidity pressure move the
price away from a level at which daytime liquidity providers are willing to
trade; as information asymmetry and inventory risk decline through the regular
session, the pressure is partially unwound. Lou et al. document cross-period
reversal, Berkman et al. document opening pressure in the largest stocks, Akbas
et al. document the positive-overnight/negative-daytime tug-of-war sequence, and
Lu et al. provide a modern liquidity-provider explanation that remains relevant
for large/liquid stocks.

The continuation evidence is therefore not ignored; it is rejected for this
candidate because it is less directly tied to the desired overnight-to-regular-
session mechanism and would be more easily confused with existing close-to-close
momentum. The current scanner also makes a pure illiquidity trade unattractive:
its `$20m` median-dollar-volume floor, `$5` price floor, exchange restrictions,
and `50`-bps spread-quality ceiling remove the most fragile tail. That may
attenuate, but does not logically eliminate, the effect because large/liquid
evidence exists. Miwa's stronger-when-illiquid result and Della Corte's explicit
warning about low-liquidity overnight trading make 2x/3x cost survival a hard
requirement.

## Implication for the ParayaBuck design

The final hypothesis is a simple long-only contrarian score on the latest
market-adjusted overnight price move, with one session of latency imposed by
the current completed-daily-bar/next-open engine. The one-session horizon is the
primitive close-to-open interval, not a copied “best” published parameter. The
candidate is deliberately not a same-open auction strategy: its executable
claim is limited to the provider-defined daily open proxy described in the data
and causality audits.
