# Independent design red-team — 2026-09-22

This review attempted to falsify the proposed *child-only, post-regular-way, next-open, long-only, six-name* expression. It did not inspect local candidate outcomes. A published return is described only to test whether the external hypothesis transfers to that expression.

| Attack | Primary evidence and implication | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Takeover contamination | Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge, *Journal of Financial Economics* 33 (1993), sample 1965–1988, [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304405X9390009Z) says abnormal long-run performance was limited to firms involved in takeover activity. A rule cannot select eventual takeover targets at entry. | Fatal to treating the famous overall child result as standalone proof. |
| Ex-ante replication and publication decay | McConnell, Ozbilgin and Wahal, *Journal of Business* 74 (2001), sample 1989–1995, [author full text](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf) explicitly tests the earlier strategy on a later sample. The child evidence is weaker and does not establish a stable six-name rule. | Open counterevidence; the positive classic estimate cannot be inherited uncritically. |
| Initial listing effects | Seifert and Rubin, *Journal of Economics and Business* 41 (1989), [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0148619589900027), reports child losses after separate trading begins. Full text was not accessible in this pass; the abstract is a counter-lead, not a quantified result for this decision. | Requires precise entry clock and post-entry evidence. |
| Modern-event substitution | Owers and Sergi, *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 8 (2021), U.S. sample 2007–2017, [open full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-021-00807-9), studies announcement, ex-dividend, and announcement-to-distribution reactions. Its five analyses do not measure the later outsider-owned child's long-only drift. | Cannot satisfy modern child-post-entry validation despite recent sample dates. |
| Small-cap and liquidity dependence | The proposal's $20m trailing median-dollar-volume gate and six slots require results specifically for a liquid child subset. Historical all-event equal-weight means do not prove the liquid subset, and a 20-day scanner window can delay entry. | Unresolved, potentially fatal. |
| Survivorship and delisting | A credible cohort must include failed/delisted children and retain the historical child entity. Current ticker lists or continuing listings are insufficient. The canonical files lack a spin-event relation. | Unresolved source and mapping gate. |
| When-issued leakage | Distribution date, when-issued trading, and regular-way trading are separate. Issuer-specific schedules can put regular-way trading on the distribution day or later. A return clock starting at the first trade or its close does not prove an achievable next-open fill. | Unresolved timing gate. |
| Future-outcome conditioning | Focus improvement, later takeovers, bankruptcy survival, index entry, and eventual operating success cannot define initial eligibility. | Reject any rank or cohort that uses them. |
| Event-definition ambiguity | Pro-rata distributions, split-offs, carve-out IPOs, partial distributions, merger-linked issues, and multiple classes have different economic and mapping paths. | A broad spin-off label is insufficient. |
| Stale ticker / security identity | CIK identifies an issuer, not its specific ordinary share class. Historical ticker/entity, listing venue and regular-way status need dated evidence; ambiguous matches fail closed. | Unresolved hard gate. |
| Diversified-only expression | Academic all-event portfolios can contain tens of children. No observed all-child mean proves the top six, much less the top one. | Fatal unless an ex-ante ranking is independently supported. |
| Long slot occupation | A 22–36 month published expression occupies scarce positions through later events. A shorter 3–6 month version requires its own evidence; changing the horizon for architecture fit would be outcome-free but still unsupported. | Unresolved, potentially fatal. |
| Generic style overlap | Newly listed children may differ informationally from V3 while still loading on size, value, liquidity or distress. Structural distinctness is not return complementarity. | Portfolio contribution unproven. |

The narrow child/post-distribution family is *definitionally* different from the earlier generic spin-off family: it isolates the child security, excludes parent and combined portfolios and announcement/when-issued reactions, and starts only after verified regular-way availability. That precision does not establish a positive, modern, liquid, concentrated child-long return. The burden of proof remains on those gates.
