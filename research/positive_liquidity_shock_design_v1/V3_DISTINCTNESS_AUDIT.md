# V3 distinctness audit

V3 ranks a signed, medium-horizon SPY-residual return trend, requires positive
12-1 residual momentum, combines 6/9/12 residual-return ranks, rejects high
IVOL and sizes inversely to residual volatility on a weekly cadence.

Published `LIQU` is monthly and unsigned with respect to return direction. It
measures a change in daily price impact, `abs(return)/dollar volume`, relative
to the same security's prior 12 monthly values. It is therefore a distinct
source formula and cadence.

External cross-sectional evidence retains liquidity shock after momentum,
short-term reversal, IVOL, current illiquidity, turnover and size controls.
Han, Hu and Huang further report that illiquidity-shock information subsumes
much IVOL-shock predictability rather than the reverse.

Mechanical overlap remains material. A favorable shock can arise from lower
absolute daily movement even when dollar volume is unchanged. That creates a
low-realized-volatility channel that V3's IVOL rejection and inverse-volatility
sizing may already prefer. The structural audit quantifies contemporaneous
score relationships to absolute-return change and volatility, without using
future returns.

Verdict: source identity is distinct from V3, but that distinction does not
establish concentrated incremental alpha. No V3 conditioning is added to the
hypothesis.
