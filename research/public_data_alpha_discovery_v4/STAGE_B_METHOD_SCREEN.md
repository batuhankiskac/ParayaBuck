# Stage B primary-method screen

6 Stage-A families retained enough primary-source context for a method audit. The other 3 failed method/long-leg/concentration gates at Stage A.

- **V4-007 dividend initiation** — REJECT_CONCENTRATION: Classic diversified event evidence; no modern liquid few-name strength rule or tail evidence recovered.
- **V4-010 forward stock split announcement** — REJECT_EVENT_TIMING: Published evidence disputes durable drift and attributes short duration to trading frictions; no robust causal post-entry concentrated long case.
- **V4-021 abnormal institutional ownership** — REJECT_CONCENTRATION: Published abstract reports high-minus-low only; no independently evidenced few-name high tail. Characteristic model and historical 13F security mapping remain unresolved.
- **V4-022 institutional breadth change** — REJECT_CONCENTRATION: Primary long-short result uses diversified deciles; no validated few-name high-breadth tail after 13F disclosure.
- **V4-031 Phase III failed-trial rebound** — REJECT_CONCENTRATION: 2025 paper has only 92 events and the long-friendly rebound is small-firm failure dependent; liquid, few-name post-disclosure evidence absent.
- **V4-033 FDA fast-track designation** — REJECT_CONCENTRATION: Recent 25-event study reports long-horizon gains but has a narrow 2019-2020 sample; liquid few-name replication, PIT sponsor mapping and independent modern confirmation absent.

Abnormal ownership: Kirk (2025 online/2026 issue), 1984–2022, 2,287,907 firm-months, characteristic-based residual ownership, 45-day public filing lag, following-month high-minus-low 76 basis points (external_published_evidence). The accessible text does not establish high-leg absolute or few-name tail. Breadth: Chen, Hong and Stein (2002) use mutual-fund holdings and diversified deciles, with 6- and 12-month long-short evidence (external_published_evidence); SEC 13F is not automatically method-equivalent. Phase III: Afik, Lahav and Zaguri (2025), 92 events; failure response and small-firm rebound dominate. Dividend initiation: Michaely, Thaler and Womack (1995) is old and lacks a modern concentrated liquid proof. Forward split: Boehme and Danielsen (2007) dispute lasting post-announcement drift, although later split-history work reports a positive aggregate effect, so the evidence is conflicting. Fast Track: Williamson and Spicer (2023) examine only 25 biotech designation announcements from June 2019–June 2020; published long-horizon estimates are external_published_evidence, but few-name liquid replication and a rank are absent.
