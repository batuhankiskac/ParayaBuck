# Future-completion dependence

Research date: 2026-09-22. Literature interpretation only; no local event returns or completion-conditioned economic sample constructed.

## Requested binary verdict and its scope

`POST_ANNOUNCEMENT_RETURNS_NOT_DEPENDENT_ON_FUTURE_COMPLETION`.

This means historical unconditional announcement-cohort evidence can be defined without selecting future completers. It is NOT proof of modern outsider-investable alpha, or of economic independence between later company actions and realized returns. The alternative verdict, `RETURNS_REQUIRE_FUTURE_REPURCHASE_OUTCOME_INFORMATION`, would overstate the evidence: future-event attribution alone does not establish that advance knowledge is necessary to hold an unconditional cohort.

## Credibility is causal only when already public

Open-market authorization is permission rather than a commitment to execute. Bonaime (2012) finds lagged completion rates associated with current completion and announcement returns. This supports historical reputation as potentially public information, not access to the new program's future completion. It also does not establish a post-next-open trading premium. [Primary journal article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/repurchases-reputation-and-returns/2192A7F94F7CE8A16681923DD7742E94).

If reputation were ever used, each historical completion observation would require its own disclosure timestamp before the current announcement. A program whose ultimate completion is learned afterward must remain unknown at entry. Aggregate issuer repurchases must not be silently assigned to an individual overlapping authorization. No credibility signal is frozen here.

## Strong adverse mechanism evidence

Bargeron, Bonaime, and Thomas (2017) find insignificant abnormal performance between authorization and completion; standard long-window gains instead occur after completion and largely at subsequent authorization or takeover announcements. Their page 494 explicitly distinguishes this ex-post source decomposition from designing an implementable trading strategy. The evidence challenges smooth drift from the initial authorization, but does not turn later realized events into permissible announcement-time filters. [Primary paper, pp. 491–494](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC/S0022109017000084a.pdf/timing_and_source_of_longrun_returns_following_repurchases.pdf).

Consequently, do not select future completers, future high-intensity repurchasers, later takeover targets, or issuers that later reauthorize. Do not exit retrospectively at the last actual repurchase. Entering after a public completion release would be a different event family, not a repair of this initial-announcement hypothesis.

The design remains rejected on modern concentration/slot evidence even though the narrowly defined no-lookahead selection gate is not itself a proven universal failure. The causal question and the economic mechanism question must remain separate.
