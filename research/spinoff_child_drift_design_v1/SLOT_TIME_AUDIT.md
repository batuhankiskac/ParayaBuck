# Horizon and scarce slot-time

Retrieved 2026-09-22. Published horizons are not interchangeable with a shorter horizon selected to free portfolio capacity.

| Original study | Child return clock and reported intervals | Implication |
|---|---|---|

| [McConnell and Ovtchinnikov (2004)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228289308_Predictability_of_Long-Term_Spin-Off_Returns) | Initial listing; 12, 24, 36-month child abnormal results plus monthly profile. | Positive 12-month result in overlapping historical sample does not prove a six-month or three-month holding rule. |
| [McConnell, Sibley and Xu (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf) | Daily CRSP returns **after ex-date**; Exhibit 3 reports 6, 12, 22, 27, 36 months. All-child excess reaches +17.1% at 22 months and rises through month 27, which authors explicitly identify as hindsight-optimal. Their active comparison portfolio uses 22 months based on prior 1965–2000 evidence. | This is a 22-month position occupation, not a 6–12-month drift proof. Positive skew makes omission/replacement risky. |

Neither 1 nor 3 months has a primary, child-specific abnormal-return estimate in these foundational papers. The initial forced-selling story could even predict early weakness; it does not itself license a three-month holding period. The 6–12-month discovery horizon is therefore not a frozen choice.

At an indicative 1–2 new names per month, a 22-month all-child strategy would occupy roughly 22–44 slots in steady state before exclusions; this is an **arithmetic scenario**, not a measured current inventory. The 2015 original study actually observed 10–33 concurrent children. A six-slot cap must discard many events or replace them early. A one-slot satellite blocks all subsequent candidates until expiry unless a published ex-ante rank can justify replacement. No such rank or shorter, adequately supported child-return window has been verified. The correct architectural response is to reject the current concentrated slot design, not shorten the holding period for convenience.
