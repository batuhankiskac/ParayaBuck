# Modern evidence and contradiction review

Review date: 2026-09-22. This is a bounded primary-literature search, not an exhaustive systematic review or a local replication. Publication year and last observation year are recorded separately. No prices or event panels were acquired for economic testing.

## Directly relevant contrary evidence

### Fu and Huang (2016): disappearance in 2003–2012

*The Persistence of Long-Run Abnormal Returns Following Stock Repurchases and Offerings*, Management Science 62(4), 964–984. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2150); [institutional accepted-version record and abstract](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/3225/).

The abstract explicitly reports disappearance of abnormal post-event returns for 2003–2012 repurchases and offerings, associated with institutional investment, liquidity, trading costs and governance/disclosure changes. This directly opposes unconditional persistence. Its trading-cost explanation concerns the market environment; it does not constitute a cost simulation of our proposed execution. Full text download failed, so table coefficients, exact sub-universe tests and standard errors remain unverified. The institutional record contains inconsistent volume/year metadata; use the publisher DOI and journal citation above, not the repository's volume 64 metadata. Do not extrapolate this null into proof that every conditional strategy fails after 2020.

### Lee, Park and Pearson (2020): motives and eras differ

*Repurchases after being well known as good news*, Journal of Corporate Finance 62, 101552. [Publisher source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0929119919309368); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcorpfin.2019.101552).

The publisher's indexed primary text describes SDC announcements during 1994–2014, split into 1994–2001, 2002–2006 and 2007–2014. Post-2001 long-horizon performance weakens; repeated repurchases become more prevalent and evidence on risk changes is mixed. The introductory discussion gives five-day announcement responses of 2.00%, 1.19% and 1.24% across those eras. Those windows are not outsider entry returns. Its regime and changing-motive evidence undermines an invariant undervaluation interpretation. Direct page opening returned 403, although substantial publisher text was searchable; exact long-run tables were not verified. Publication in 2020 does not supply post-2020 observations.

### Bargeron, Bonaime and Thomas (2017): later events, not smooth drift

*The Timing and Source of Long-Run Returns Following Repurchases*, JFQA 52, 491–517. [Publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC/S0022109017000084a.pdf/timing_and_source_of_longrun_returns_following_repurchases.pdf).

Section III/p.496 and Table 1: 19,498 authorizations from January 1979–September 2010, with completion announcements searched from January 1980–September 2010. The abstract/p.491 finds returns between authorization and completion indistinguishable from zero, with subsequent abnormal performance concentrated around later authorizations and takeover attempts. This challenges treating a four-year average as continuous drift available over any shorter horizon. Future-event conditioning is explanatory, not a permissible initial filter. Failure to announce completion cannot justify deleting an observation after seeing its return. This paper is a strong mechanism/horizon challenge, but its observation period does not settle the post-2020 question.

## Supportive evidence and its limits

### Evgeniou, Junqué de Fortuny, Nassuphis and Vermaelen (2018)

*Volatility and the buyback anomaly*, Journal of Corporate Finance 49, 32–53. [Published primary text](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0929119917307022); [author manuscript, dated May 13, 2016](https://tevgeniou.github.io/BuybacksIssuers/manuscript.pdf); [author project](https://tevgeniou.github.io/BuybacksIssuers/).

The published article reports survival under Fama–French five-factor and Stambaugh–Yuan four-factor models, especially among volatile stocks. The directly read manuscript (pp.3–4) covers 1985–2015 and explicitly contradicts Fu/Huang's disappearance interpretation. Crucially, p.4 says value-weighting all events removes calendar-time alpha except for high-volatility stocks; detailed value-weighted results are described as available on request. This is evidence of a size/weighting limitation even in a supportive study. Manuscript and final publication are different versions; manuscript numbers are not represented as final-table replication. Neither an aggregate pre/post sample nor high-volatility conditional results prove a current large/liquid one-slot strategy. No code from the author project was executed or imported.

### Ahmed, Taffler and Tosun (2025 online; 2026 issue)

*Executive compensation and the credibility of share buyback announcements*, Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting 66, 119–154. [Open-access primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11156-025-01398-1).

Section 3 reports 6,403 observations/2,147 firms during 1992–2019 after linking SDC, ExecuComp, CRSP and Compustat; ExecuComp links to S&P 1500 coverage. Section 5.5/Table 8 studies conditional longer-term returns: better incentive alignment relates to higher one-year abnormal returns, while the text explicitly acknowledges smaller returns in recent periods. This prevents a blanket claim that all recent research finds zero. It remains pooled, conditional evidence rather than a demonstrated post-2010-only or post-2020 net-cost strategy. The average 1.45% CAR in Section 3/Table 2 uses (−1,+1), not a next-open entry. Compensation-data publication timing and missing-data selection would need independent PIT validation before use; no such feature is approved here.

### Guest, Kothari and Venkat (2023): related, different estimand

*Share repurchases on trial: Large-sample evidence on share price performance, executive compensation, and corporate investment*, Financial Management 52, 19–40. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fima.12415); [authors' SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4149796).

The primary abstract finds modest appreciation without average reversal and challenges claims of systematic manipulation or investment crowding-out. Full sample dates and outsider next-open tables were not verified from accessible primary text here. This is relevant counterevidence to an assertion that buybacks generally harm shareholders, but is not accepted as a modern announcement-drift strategy replication. A corporate-finance welfare question and executable abnormal return are distinct.

## Search outcomes that cannot fill the US gate

- Dutta (2015), [primary publisher PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/23322039.2015.1119461), abstract/p.1: 63 Indian buybacks, July 2008–June 2012, method-sensitive results. Despite its relevant title, it is not a US replication.
- *Automatic Identification and Classification of Share Buybacks and their Effect on Short-, Mid- and Long-Term Returns* (2022), [author research text](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.12863): international thesis/preprint data include 2022 through April and only 14.7% US announcements (data overview, country distribution/Figure 4). Figures 9–12 show sensitivity to size benchmarks and transaction-size selection. It is not a clean US large/liquid post-2020 next-open/cost replication. No classifier or return model from it is adopted.
- Tender-offer studies, issuer execution timing, net payout/issuance factors, and buyback ETF performance were not substituted for the specified public authorization event.

## Evidence matrix and gate implication

| Requirement | What was established | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|
| Historical announcement response | Positive historical CARs, including some larger-company samples | Response already realized before entry is not tradable alpha |
| Post-publication persistence | Literature disagreement; supportive and null studies both exist | Common modern universe, event definition and benchmark reconciliation |
| Observations after 2010 | Studies extending through 2012, 2014, 2015 and 2019 | Pooled full-period significance does not establish an isolated modern regime |
| Post-2020 US evidence | No qualifying primary replication verified in this bounded search | This is an evidence gap, not a demonstrated economic null |
| Large/liquid firms | Some historical/conditional large-company coverage; supportive manuscript flags lost VW aggregate alpha | Explicit liquid-universe positive result under permitted execution/costs |
| Long-only next-open net return | Not established by the retrieved results | Avoid benchmark-short or event-window substitutions |
| One/six-slot feasibility | Not established | Finite positions, overlapping events, idle cash, exposure and concentration |

**Design judgment:** the literature supplies a plausible historical conditional hypothesis and serious modern relevance objections. It does not clear a modern large/liquid, next-open, long-only feasibility gate. If that gate requires affirmative evidence, record the missing proof; do not disguise it as a replicated rejection. Conversely, older positive averages and a recent publication date cannot support READY status. Cost headroom, publication latency, a causal event universe and allocator feasibility still require their own authorized stage. This review neither launches that stage nor authorizes PAPER/LIVE promotion.
