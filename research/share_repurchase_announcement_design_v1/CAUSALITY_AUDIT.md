# Causality and V3 distinctness

No local outcome was inspected for this candidate. These are design
constraints and literature-based inferences, not regression results.

## Information chain

Board decision -> first demonstrable public disclosure -> validated public
availability -> first regular tradable open strictly afterward.

A confidential board date is never the public timestamp. An SEC Accepted time
can establish a conservative information floor for material in that filing,
subject to actual dissemination semantics; it cannot retroactively move a
press release's information to an earlier private approval. If the filing
repeats an earlier release, retain the prior release identity and original
public time when verifiable. If not verifiable, mark event-age uncertainty;
do not pretend the later filing is a fresh announcement.

Entry must be after public availability with a documented session calendar,
time zone, holidays, halts and ties. A release at exactly the open cannot
receive that same open. No intraday timing optimization is contemplated.
A next-open rule is causally implementable in principle; published
announcement-window effects are not evidence of its profitability.

## Confounding disclosures

An earnings-release repurchase authorization bundles cash-flow news, guidance,
dividends and the buyback. Raw announcement reactions do not identify the
buyback's causal contribution. The primary filing sample explicitly records
bundling rather than deleting these cases after seeing their outcomes.

Separating earnings surprise would require historical expectations measured
before the release or an independently justified isolated-announcement
protocol. Neither is demonstrated here. Excluding earnings releases after
inspecting performance would be a different hypothesis; no such exclusion or
new candidate is frozen.

Subsequent takeovers, actual execution and later authorizations can explain
realized gains without being knowable initial selectors. Bargeron, Bonaime and
Thomas document step-like future-event contributions; their decomposition
is not an entry rule. See `FUTURE_COMPLETION_DEPENDENCE.md` for the precise
scope of the binary completion gate and its primary source.

## Distinctness from V3

The proposed information object is an issuer disclosure, whereas current V3
uses price-history residual momentum and a positive formation-score gate.
This is structural input distinctness only. A past-decline buyback subset may
also contain value, size, reversal and subsequent momentum exposures.

External factor-adjusted studies reviewed in `PRIMARY_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`
and `MODERN_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md` provide tests against conventional factors,
with mixed modern results. Such controls do not replicate the local V3
strategy, its point-in-time pool or its six-slot allocation. There is no
verified modern incremental concentrated long leg beyond momentum, value,
size and prior returns for the requested rule. No V3 score is added and no
local V3 outcome correlation, overlap-return study or combination is run.

## Fail-closed requirements

Missing public-time authority, stale disclosure, ambiguous share class,
unresolved CIK/entity mapping, mixed execution methods, uncertain increments
and duplicate announcements must be identified before any economic dataset
could be built. Do not infer these attributes from subsequent returns,
completion or present company metadata.
