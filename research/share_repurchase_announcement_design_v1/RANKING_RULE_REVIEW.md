# Announcement-time ranking review

Research date: 2026-09-22. Pre-outcome literature/design review; no local price data, returns, ranking calculations, or candidate economics used.

## Verdict

`NO_DEFENSIBLE_REPURCHASE_CONCENTRATION_RULE`.

This is a verdict on the requested modern, concentrated, long-only implementation, not a claim that repurchase characteristics have no historical predictive information. No ranking is frozen.

## Strongest simple lead: prior price decline

Peyer and Vermaelen (2009) relate the anomaly to overreaction to bad news. Their prior-return analysis and Figure 5 provide a historical rationale to prefer beaten-down announcers. However, the explicit concentrated demonstration selects 50 stocks annually using a multi-characteristic undervaluation index, rather than the single strongest decliner. Its ingredients include size, book-to-market, prior returns, and stated motivation. That demonstration does not establish a one-variable top-one or top-six rule. [Primary paper, Table 4 and Figure 5](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn024); [indexed full-paper copy](https://www.dimfunds.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/08.03.27-Nature-of-Buyback-Anomalies.pdf).

The six-month prior-decline concept is the ONE simple lead evaluated for possible selection. It is information available before the event and avoids a market-cap denominator. It is not adopted: a historical sorted cohort does not demonstrate modern liquid-stock alpha at the extreme rank. No numerical lookback endpoint, rank cutoff, tie-break, or blended score is invented to bridge that gap. A fully specified candidate requires stronger external support first.

## Other characteristics are not substitutes

| Characteristic | Interpretation and limitation |
|---|---|
| Authorization amount / market capitalization | Measures announced scope, not completed purchases. Requires historical public shares/market cap; the supplied repository constraint says no canonical PIT market-cap panel exists. Today's shares cannot fill that gap. A large dollar amount alone mostly ranks issuer size. |
| U-index | A composite, therefore outside the user's no-blend rule; size and valuation also require PIT data. Treat the published index as contrary evidence to extrapolating one simple variable, not permission to recreate it. |
| Stated undervaluation | Public but discretionary language. A categorical statement does not supply an independently supported ordinal top-one selector. |
| Prior completed-program credibility | Bonaime links lagged completion to current completion and announcement reactions; this is not evidence of modern post-disclosure long-only ranking alpha. Past disclosures could be causal, but later-completed programs cannot be backfilled into historical reputation. [Bonaime (2012)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/repurchases-reputation-and-returns/2192A7F94F7CE8A16681923DD7742E94). |

Fu and Huang's accepted paper reports disappearance of average long-run abnormal returns in 2003–2012. That does not disprove every conditional rule, but it prevents carrying the older prior-decline relation straight into a modern implementation. [Institutional primary manuscript record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/3225/).

There is contrary supportive evidence: Evgeniou and coauthors' May 2016 author manuscript, using announcements through 2015, finds persistent abnormal performance and positive relations with pre-announcement volatility. It also reports that aggregate value-weighted calendar-time alpha disappears, with a high-volatility exception. Its enhanced U-index is again a blend. This prevents a blanket disappearance claim, but does not validate prior decline alone in the required one/six-slot setting. [Primary author manuscript, pp. 3–6](https://tevgeniou.github.io/BuybacksIssuers/manuscript.pdf).

No authorization-size rule, completion filter, V3 score, or alternative blend is promoted because the simplest lead lacks sufficient evidence. This is a hypothesis/concentration failure independently of the market-cap data dependency.
