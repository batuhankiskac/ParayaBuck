# Primary literature review — child shares after separation

Retrieved 2026-09-22. This review treats a published return as evidence only for its stated security, sample, benchmark, and return clock. The older studies used CRSP, Compustat, CCH/Mergent, or other commercial archives; those datasets are evidence provenance, not approved implementation inputs.

| Original study | Population and child clock | What the full text establishes | Limitation for this candidate |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Seifert and Rubin, *Spin-offs and the listing phenomena*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0148619589900027), *Journal of Economics and Business* 41 (Feb 1989), 1–19 | Parent and child after separate trading starts | Publisher abstract says children incur abnormal losses once trading separately. | Full article is paywalled here; sample dates, horizon, and exact table cannot be verified. This is counterevidence, not a quantified input. |
| [Cusatis, Miles and Woolridge, *Restructuring through spinoffs: The stock market evidence*](https://longrunplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/restructuring-through-spinoffs.pdf), *JFE* 33 (June 1993), 293–311; [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(93)90009-Z) | 1965–1988, 146 newly traded, voluntary, fully nontaxable children; first trading-day **close** to 6/12/24/36 months. Their 815 initial distributions narrow to 231 nontaxable voluntary, then 146 pure listed children; 89/146 initially OTC. | Table 3 child **equal-event-weight mean** raw buy-and-hold returns: 7.7%, 19.9%, 52.0%, 76.0%; industry/size matched excess: −1.0%, 4.5%, 25.0%, 33.6%. Only 24/36-month matched excess is significant at 5%. Child is distinct from parent or market-value-weighted parent–child combined result. Paper retains delisted/taken-over children using last available price, but does not establish modern delisting-return completeness. It attributes long-run abnormal returns primarily to subsequent takeovers; excluding takeover-involved firms makes most intervals insignificant. | Cannot infer a next-open return from day-one-close returns. The strongest effect is late and takeover-contaminated. The OTC majority undermines direct transfer to a liquid listed subset. |
| [Desai and Jain, *Firm performance and focus*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X9900032X), *JFE* 54 (Oct 1999), 75–101 | 155 U.S. completed spins, 1975–1991; up to 36 months after spin-off. | Publisher text establishes larger long-run abnormal returns for focus-increasing events and separate parent/child analysis. The focus classification is an ex-ante event characteristic; it is a cohort split, not a top-six ranking demonstration. | Full numeric subsidiary table was not accessible from the publisher during this pass. Reported child numbers in secondary reproductions are not promoted to verified primary findings here. |
| [McConnell, Ozbilgin and Wahal, *Spin-offs, Ex Ante*](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf), *Journal of Business* 74 (Apr 2001), 245–280 | 1989–1995 replication; 96 pure children. Child return clock starts at initial listing date; industry/size matched buy-and-hold and size/book-to-market and FF3 alternatives. | At 24 months child industry/size matched excess is 5.8% (t = 0.29), median 0%; at 36 months −20.9% (t = −0.75), median 2%. A delayed 7–24 month strategy is −3.3%, 7–36 is −21.3%. Conversely, size/book-to-market portfolio benchmarking yields 23.9% at 24 months (bootstrap p = 0.02), 26.9% at 36 months (p = 0.09). FF3 alpha and industry/size matching do not robustly reject zero. Delisted securities remain through last available return. | The inference is benchmark- and horizon-sensitive; one cannot label the entire replication either a clean success or a clean failure. No six-name selection tested. |
| [McConnell and Ovtchinnikov, *Predictability of Long-Term Spinoff Returns*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228289308_Predictability_of_Long-Term_Spin-Off_Returns), *Journal of Investment Management* 2(3) (2004), 35–44; author-uploaded full working text | 311 pure, tax-free pro-rata children from 1965–2000; initial listing date onward. | Author-uploaded full text reports positive child average excess over 12/24/36 months on industry/size and size/book-to-market benchmarks; at 12 months, 19.40% and 16.08%, respectively. It frames a diversified calendar-time portfolio, not a high-conviction six-name rule. | Overlaps the earlier samples substantially, so not independent post-publication validation. Commercial databases used. |
| [McConnell, Sibley and Xu, *The Stock Price Performance of Spin-Off Subsidiaries, Their Parents, and the Spin-Off ETF, 2001–2013*](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf), *Journal of Portfolio Management* 42(1) (Fall 2015), 143–152; author-hosted full text | 146 events, 153 children, Jan 2001–Dec 2012 originations; U.S. nontaxable non-REIT. Daily return accumulation starts **day after ex-date**, per article's March 17/March 18 example. | At 22 months, mean child raw 31.7%, 25-cell size/book-to-market benchmark 14.6%, difference 17.1%; 36-month raw 49.4%, benchmark 22.8%. Mean driven materially by right skew. Its live-style all-child portfolio contains 10–33 names (26 initially) with 22-month holding. | Latest spin origin is 2012 despite 2015 publication/2013 return endpoint. Day-after-ex-date close-to-close arithmetic is not a proved next-open fill. No top-six, top-one, liquid-subset, or post-2015 cohort test. |

The narrow child, post-listing question is materially more precise than generic spin-off value creation. It also faces a stricter burden: announcement value creation, parent/combined gains, and pre-distribution price discovery are outside its return window. The historical long-only signal is real in several samples but not portable to a concentrated, liquid, next-open design without further independent evidence.
