# Natural ex-ante ranking review

**No externally validated, simple rank justifies choosing the best six or best one from contemporaneous U.S. spin-off children.** Retrieved 2026-09-22. A group-level association is not assumed to be monotonic within the group.

| Candidate variable | Primary evidence and mechanism | Why it does not pass as a child rank |
|---|---|---|
| Focus-increasing separation | [Desai and Jain (1999), publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X9900032X) associate improved focus with better announcement and long-run performance in their 1975–91 sample. The distinction can sometimes be coded from disclosed businesses/industries before entry. | Binary event cohort; no accessible primary table showing that *most focused* children beat other focused children or that selecting six among them works. Classifications may be ambiguous for multi-segment firms. Focus is a possible cohort restriction, not a validated tie-breaker. |
| Child market value / liquidity | [Cusatis et al. (1993)](https://longrunplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/restructuring-through-spinoffs.pdf) have 89/146 initially OTC; [McConnell et al. (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf) give child size distributions but an all-child return. The [Copenhagen thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/98732261/1642425_MastersThesis_Final.pdf) reports large-cap child results based on 11 observations, without statistical significance. | Liquidity is needed for executability; no verified monotonic alpha relation licenses ranking more liquid children above less liquid ones. Size may change risk and trading cost while eroding the original small-child exposure. |
| Relative distribution size | [Veld and Veld-Merkoulova's announcement meta-analysis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2370.2008.00243.x) reports higher **announcement** abnormal returns for larger spin-offs. | Parent announcement result occurs before the proposed child entry; no child post-listing monotonic return evidence. |
| Institutional forced selling / roadshows | [Ryan (2017), Michigan dissertation](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/140927) finds pre-spin communication relates to post-spin shareholder retention and price pressure; [Seifert and Rubin (1989)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0148619589900027) find initial child losses. | Mechanism is plausible but direct causal ranking would need PIT institutional base, mandate mismatch, and pre-entry communication measures with verified future child-return discrimination. The accessible thesis abstract alone does not supply such a validated rank. |
| Insider buying | [2020 *Journal of Accounting and Public Policy* original article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278425419300213) associates post-spin insider net buying and 1-year child excess returns. | Purchases are measured during three months **after** spin; using them to rank at the first regular-way open leaks future information. A later-entry design would be a different hypothesis and horizon. |
| Recency × liquidity | Discovery suggestion; no primary study identified here uses this expression to rank children. | Recency is occupancy/expiry metadata; liquidity is execution eligibility. Multiplying them creates an unsupported score. |

The only defensible rule from the strongest 2015 full-text evidence is **buy all** qualifying children for its reported horizon. Imposing six slots creates a choice problem for which the literature supplies no validated ex-ante ordering. No weighted conviction score is proposed. **Ranking/concentration verdict: `DESIGN_REJECT_NO_DEFENSIBLE_HYPOTHESIS` for the requested architecture**, irrespective of whether source and mapping audits ultimately pass.
