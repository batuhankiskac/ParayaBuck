# Primary activism literature review

## Brav, Jiang, Partnoy, and Thomas (2008)

[Journal of Finance article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x), sample 2001–2006.

The often-cited roughly 7% abnormal return is an announcement-centered window and is not an outsider's post-publication return. Table VI is the relevant separation: the equal-weighted months +1 to +3 portfolio has 1.093% monthly alpha (t=2.01), while the value-weighted counterpart is 0.141% (t=0.28). Later value-weighted windows are insignificant. The authors explicitly find the favorable response concentrated in smaller targets. Deal-lifetime returns begin before public activism and end at activist exit; they cannot be treated as a public next-open rule.

## Greenwood and Schor (2009)

[Primary paper](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Investor%20Activism%20and%20Takeovers_0dfbaa36-4370-4880-9170-6ad3d60249da.pdf), sample 1993–2006.

Their cross-section divides sharply by a future fact. Targets acquired within 18 months accumulate large positive returns; targets remaining independent do not show reliably positive long-horizon abnormal returns. About 18.1% of activist targets were acquired within one year versus 7.2% of matched firms. Their 11 percentage-point increment multiplied by a 30% takeover premium gives about 3.3%, comparable with the observed announcement effect. The study does not authorize filtering for future acquisitions at filing time.

## Bebchuk, Brav, and Jiang (2015)

[Published paper](https://www.columbialawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Bebchuk-Brav-Jiang.pdf); [NBER version](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21227/w21227.pdf).

This paper is evidence against a later reversal, not evidence of reliably positive post-filing alpha. Its calendar-time alphas for months +1 to +36 and +1 to +60 are statistically insignificant in both equal- and value-weighted portfolios; the value-weighted +1 to +60 estimate is slightly negative. “No long-term harm” and “a profitable new outsider entry” are distinct hypotheses.

## Becht, Franks, Grant, and Wagner (2017)

[Review of Financial Studies article](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/30/9/2933/3852480); [working paper](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/ssrn-id23762716_1.pdf). The sample contains 1,740 engagements in 23 countries during 2000–2010.

Initial-disclosure event windows and outcome announcements are positive, especially takeover outcomes. Engagements without outcomes have weak or negative performance. The authors' decomposition identifies disclosure months and outcome months as non-investable to an outsider; the all-other-month Carhart results are insignificant or negative. Public outcome returns cannot be attributed to a filing-time selector without knowing which outcome will occur.

## Evidence taxonomy

Every major source is decomposed below. “Not isolated” means the paper's design does not identify that component separately; it does not mean the component is zero.

| Primary source and decisive location | Pre-filing accumulation | Filing / announcement | Immediate post-filing | Subsequent engagement period | Future outcome / takeover | Outsider after dissemination |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brav et al. (2008), event study and Table VI | Included in the broad filing-centered window; activist block already accumulated | Roughly 7% familiar result combines the disclosure-centered window | Table VI months +1 to +3: EW 1.093% monthly (t=2.01), VW 0.141% (t=0.28) | Later VW +4–6, +7–9, +10–12 estimates are insignificant; deal holding period begins before public activism | Sale-of-company demand has the largest announcement response; later outcome is not an entry-time fact | Only the +1 onward portfolio is conceptually available; its favorable result does not survive value weighting |
| Greenwood & Schor (2009), Tables 4 and 6, pp. 369 and 372 | Event series starts before/at filing; not an outsider-only slice | Acquired targets already have stronger filing-window response than independents | Filing-to-+1 differences are not a conservative next-open portfolio | Independent-target long-horizon abnormal returns are weak; “no news” targets are zero at +18 months | Acquired targets reach 25.85% monthly CAR at +18 months; 18.1% acquired within a year versus 7.2% controls | Unconditional residual is not shown robustly; acquisition status is future information |
| Bebchuk, Brav & Jiang (2015), Tables 7–9, pp. 1125–1130 | Their forty-day short-term window surrounds filing | Confirms initial spike; the paper's main question is reversal/harm | Not separately implemented with acceptance/dissemination/next-open timing | Calendar-time +1–36/+1–60 alphas are insignificant: EW 0.24%/0.21%, VW 0.17%/−0.03% monthly | Outcome conditioning is not a valid selector; departure analysis asks a different no-harm question | Supports “no reversal,” not reliably positive new-entry alpha |
| Becht et al. (2017), Tables 5, 7, and 8 | Initial-disclosure [-10,+10] and [-20,+20] windows include pre-disclosure days | Table 5 disclosure CAR is 6.14%/6.40% in those windows | Not isolated as a next-open strategy | Table 8 all-other-month Carhart results are insignificant or negative | Table 7: takeover outcomes 9.33%; multiple outcomes including takeover 18.3%; no-outcome portfolios are weak/negative | Authors explicitly describe disclosure- and outcome-month portfolios as non-investable for outsiders; other-month evidence is insufficient |

## Component-level conclusion

| Return component | Public outsider can capture? | Evidence conclusion |
|---|---:|---|
| Pre-filing block accumulation/leakage | No | Material part of broad event windows |
| Filing/announcement reaction | Usually not fully | None of the core studies applies a conservative dissemination-to-next-open fill |
| +1 onward | Yes in principle | Weak; the short positive EW result does not survive VW |
| Engagement-period return | Yes in principle | Often includes unforecastable outcome announcements and lacks robust other-month alpha |
| Successful outcome/takeover | Not as an ex-ante filter | Strong but future-conditioned |

## Literature verdict

`ACTIVISM_RETURNS_REQUIRE_FUTURE_OUTCOME_INFORMATION`

The primary evidence does not establish a robust, unconditional, liquid-stock, next-open post-filing long leg.
