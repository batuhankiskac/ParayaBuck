# Concentration and slot-duration feasibility

Verdict: `GUIDANCE_DRIFT_REQUIRES_DIVERSIFICATION` as a conservative description of the scope of demonstrated portfolio evidence. It does not establish that diversification is mathematically required or that concentrated implementation is impossible.

This family has a genuine continuous economic news variable, an issuer-specific disclosure, an explicitly positive good-news long leg and a three-month horizon in its primary study. Those are substantive advantages; this review does not import a rejection rationale from another event family.

## What the published tail evidence establishes

Ng, Tuna and Verdi Table 1 supports the highest surprise quintile. The final regression specification transforms Surprise into scaled quintile ranks; it is not a continuous raw-surprise Fama–MacBeth extrapolation to the single largest event. Table 5's Fama–MacBeth inference applies to PEAD-tercile/Surprise-quintile portfolios. Figure 2 explicitly describes equal weighting. No final-paper value-weighted top-tail or top-one/top-six result was verified. [Final published paper, Tables 1, 4–5 and Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-012-9217-4).

Thus, higher surprise is a natural direction for research, but the mean of an extreme quintile does not establish that its most extreme observation has the highest expected net return. Price scaling, EPS basis mismatch, small denominators and consensus measurement error can create extreme numerical ranks. Missing or incomparable data must fail closed; they cannot be repaired by selecting a different proxy after observing outcomes.

## Three months is a useful prior, not a completed allocator

The primary three-month result supplies an externally motivated horizon. A three-month position consumes approximately one-quarter of a slot-year, substantially less than a multi-year event. That is capacity arithmetic, not an alpha-per-slot-year or opportunity-cost estimate. Strong historical long-leg evidence makes this horizon worth investigating if the mandatory data and modern-evidence gates can be satisfied.

However, the paper's return-start inconsistency must be resolved before any exact freeze. A daily next-open implementation cannot be described as an exact match to a third-day academic start. No 1/3/6/12-month optimization was performed, and no alternative horizon is proposed.

## Six-slot and one-slot assessment

Six slots can hold sparse events with cash; no rule requires six simultaneous opportunities. One slot can represent one issuer's news, but its concentration risk cannot be inferred from an equal-weighted quintile. The review did not verify external evidence for monotonicity within the top quintile, a top-decile optimum, or an extreme-event capped allocator after costs. Therefore concentration readiness remains unproved rather than economically rejected.

A future pre-outcome specification would need valid historical cutoffs, deterministic handling of ties and concurrent events, one exposure per issuer, no duplicate/reaffirmation-induced extension by accident, and a fixed policy for new fiscal-period guidance during an existing holding. These are unresolved design requirements, not frozen parameters. A new quarterly forecast is not automatically a replacement for an annual forecast on a different period.
