# Design red team

Review date: 2026-09-22. Each answer distinguishes demonstrated evidence from an unmeasured economic claim.

1. **Can the exact EAR benchmark be reconstructed PIT?** Not from current repository inputs. Published daily portfolio returns do not identify a firm's contemporaneous matched portfolio.
2. **Does EAR require unavailable book-to-market membership?** The primary characteristic-adjusted definition does. Reconstructing membership needs effective-dated market equity, accounting data, link history and the paper's breakpoint rules.
3. **Is simpler market-adjusted EAR independently validated?** Earlier literature supplies an independently motivated market-adjusted announcement-return family. Its exact index, window, formation and evidence must be preserved; this does not authorize `stock − SPY` by convenience.
4. **Are earnings event dates/times complete from SEC?** No. Item 2.02 and exhibits establish a strong primary path, but release-first, call-only, late filing, foreign issuer and missing/duplicate cases prevent a completeness claim.
5. **Does SEC-based event date differ materially from Compustat RDQ?** It can. Published date-validation work documents mismatches; an SEC adaptation is a source change requiring its own coverage and alignment proof.
6. **Does the three-day window delay entry until t+2?** Yes, for a `t−1,t,t+1` statistic observed from completed daily bars.
7. **Is enough drift left after t+2 open?** Historical formation timing must be matched exactly; contemporary liquid-stock net evidence after the executable open remains unproved.
8. **Does modern US evidence support exact EAR after 2010?** The bounded primary-source review did not verify a sufficiently direct modern replication of the requested exact, executable concentrated long leg.
9. **Does it survive in large/liquid stocks?** Evidence adequate for the current scanner-like universe and execution clock was not verified.
10. **Is the long leg independently positive?** Primary evidence is reported separately from the long-short spread in the literature review. Statistical and weighting limits are retained rather than assigning the spread to the long leg.
11. **Is EAR merely event momentum?** EAR is mechanically a short return, but primary controls and comparisons can establish information beyond conventional momentum. That does not establish local portfolio complementarity.
12. **Does V3 already capture it shortly afterward?** A positive EAR can enter later residual-momentum windows. No local future-return correlation or outcome overlap was calculated; structural overlap alone cannot answer the alpha question.
13. **Is top-six concentration defensible?** Quintile portfolios and cross-sectional relations do not automatically validate the six most extreme observations after measurement error and corporate events.
14. **Is a top-one satellite defensible?** No verified within-tail monotonic or top-one evidence was found. Issuer specificity and continuous ranking are useful architecture, not proof of alpha density.
15. **Does a 60-day holding waste scarce capacity?** Roughly one quarter is operationally plausible, but slot opportunity cost and net economics are unmeasured. A horizon is not frozen after a non-READY verdict.
16. **Do repeat quarterly events complicate episodes?** Yes. A later announcement may occur before expiry and needs a predeclared replace/refresh/independent-episode rule. No favorable rule may be selected after outcomes.
17. **Does after-hours alignment create lookahead risk?** Yes. Calendar RDQ may not identify the session that first absorbs news; using a full return bar before it completes is lookahead.
18. **Are windows contaminated by other events?** EAR intentionally captures the complete earnings package. Extraordinary M&A, bankruptcy, financing, split and reorganization news can dominate; arbitrary exclusions would change the hypothesis.
19. **Does publication decay weaken the effect?** Generic anomaly-decay and PEAD evidence raises the bar, but is not direct proof the exact EAR effect vanished. Direct modern support remains insufficient.
20. **Are recent PEAD risk explanations fatal?** Not by themselves. Competing risk, attention and learning explanations affect interpretation; the design still needs a current tradable long-leg result.
21. **Does broad `US_EQUITY` matter after issuer mapping?** Issuer linkage reduces ETF/ETN contamination but does not identify the correct listed class or eliminate all products, ADR, preferred, ticker-reuse and historical-status ambiguity.
22. **Can financials and utilities be included?** Only if the primary paper's own robustness result and the chosen alternative specification support it. That can ease the missing PIT industry-classification problem but does not cure benchmark membership.
23. **Does next-open after the full window remain causally faithful?** It is causal and mechanically supported by the engine; faithfulness depends on the paper's exact formation clock and evidence remaining after that point.
24. **Are costs acceptable?** Unknown. Quarterly event turnover suggests moderate cadence, but no candidate turnover, spread, cost or outcome calculation was authorized.

The decision must not be upgraded because EAR is measurable in principle. Modern, liquid, concentrated and exact-clock evidence are conjunctive gates; benchmark and event-source feasibility remain separate data gates.
