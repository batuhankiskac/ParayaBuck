# Ranking rule review

## Candidate public variables

The literature discusses ownership percentage, demand type, sale demand, activist identity/reputation, target size, campaign structure, and multiple activists. None supplies a sufficiently validated, monotonic, pre-filing/public rule for choosing one liquid target at the next open.

- Ownership percentage can reflect conviction, control constraints, liquidity, block-building cost, or denominator mechanics; more is not established as uniformly better.
- Sale-of-company demands have larger announcement effects, but the post-publication remainder is not isolated and the return is takeover-dependent.
- Demand-type labels require Item 4 parsing and can be mixed or boilerplate.
- Activist reputation changes through time and published classifications often use later campaign history. A present-day “known activist” list would contaminate historical selection.
- Target size predicts stronger effects among smaller firms, which conflicts with the desire for liquid, capacity-relevant instruments and is not a natural one-slot score.
- Multiple-activist status can develop after the initial filing and therefore is not necessarily known at entry.

## Verdict

`NO_DEFENSIBLE_CONCENTRATION_RULE`

An equal-weight all-events design would avoid invented ranking, but it would be a different broad event portfolio, would still lack post-filing evidence, and can exceed account slots. No composite score is proposed.
