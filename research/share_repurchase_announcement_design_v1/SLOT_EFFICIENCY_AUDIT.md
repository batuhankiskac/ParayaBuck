# Slot duration and concentration

Research date: 2026-09-22. Architecture inference from external studies; no local P&L, opportunity-cost return, or return-per-slot calculation.

## Verdict

`REPURCHASE_PREMIUM_REQUIRES_DIVERSIFICATION`.

This is the required conservative design classification: the supportive evidence reviewed is portfolio evidence, and it does not validate a one-slot or six-slot implementation. It is not a mathematical claim that profits are impossible with fewer names.

## Evidence versus architecture

An announcement identifies an issuer, which is a better structural match to an event sleeve than a broad factor score. But issuer specificity alone does not establish alpha concentration. Peyer and Vermaelen's Figure 5 uses 50 selected stocks; its multi-year portfolio evidence cannot simply be resized to one or six names. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn024).

The same researchers publicly implemented a 24-stock portfolio in 2009, predominantly small issuers with approximately $500 million average market capitalization. This is supportive evidence that an outsider could act after announcements, but the contemporary author account is a selected crisis-period demonstration, not modern liquid-stock, cost-controlled, one-slot validation. [Peyer and Vermaelen's dated account](https://knowledge.insead.edu/economics-finance/buyback-investing).

Long-horizon announcement studies examine windows spanning 12–48 months. Bargeron, Bonaime, and Thomas's attribution to later discrete events makes the timing uncertain rather than a reliably harvestable monthly drift. [Primary study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC/S0022109017000084a.pdf/timing_and_source_of_longrun_returns_following_repurchases.pdf).

## Alpha density per slot-year

One position held for 12 months consumes one slot-year; a 48-month position consumes four. Six simultaneous four-year holdings commit all six slots throughout that period. These are capacity identities, not economic estimates. A credible design needs evidence that the chosen extreme-ranked issuer supplies enough persistent expected excess return to justify that commitment. Published broad-cohort averages do not establish it. Neither reducing the horizon nor choosing the largest authorization is a justified shortcut.

| Architecture question | Finding |
|---|---|
| Six-slot standalone | Operationally conceivable with cash when sparse; externally validated concentration is absent. No minimum six-event breadth requirement is imposed. |
| One-slot satellite | A single issuer adds event, execution, business and takeover uncertainty; no reviewed single-variable selector proves sufficient alpha density. No V3 outcome correlation was examined. |
| Daily next-open | Multi-year evidence does not inherently require intraday entry, but announcement-window gains cannot be credited to a later open. Event timestamp and execution-cost evidence still need their separate gates. |
| Repeated announcements | Can prolong issuer exposure and create overlapping opportunities; raw event counts do not equal independent bets or free slots. |
| Holding rule | No ONE externally justified concentrated holding rule is established. No horizon is frozen and no local horizon comparison is authorized. |

Modern disappearance evidence further reduces the basis for a long commitment: Fu and Huang report no long-run anomaly for 2003–2012, associated with changes including greater liquidity. This is counterevidence rather than a claim about every later conditional effect. [Primary accepted-manuscript record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/3225/).

Reject the requested scarce-slot thesis on current evidence. Do not claim that diversification is mechanically mandatory or that an unrun strategy has negative economics; the missing proof is sufficient concentration and duration support before local outcomes are viewed.
