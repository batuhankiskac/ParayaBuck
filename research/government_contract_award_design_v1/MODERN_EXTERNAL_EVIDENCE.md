# Modern and external evidence

Classification: LIMITED_EXTERNAL_SUPPORT for government-demand economics; no independent confirmation found of the precise absolute federal award-size, concentrated long-only, post-publication signal.

| Source | What it supports | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| [Pyun (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176525001727) | Directional large-award long-term equity association | Causal first-public timestamp and exact tradable high leg |
| [Parajuli (2023)](https://academic.oup.com/raps/article/13/1/146/6575918) | Post-disclosure predictability from material government customer sales | Individual federal award-date signal; annual 10-K sales are a different exposure |
| [Xu, Yang and You (2025)](https://www.nancyxu.net/_files/ugd/efd0b7_380b808e7ce7444da364c25d1cbf259b.pdf) | Procurement exposure can matter around subsequent earnings information | Immediate or persistent drift from award publication; results cluster at earnings |
| [Hebous and Zimmermann (2016)](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2016/12/31/can-government-demand-stimulate-private-investment-evidence-from-u-s-43784) | Federal demand can affect investment | Stock returns after announcement |
| [Falcone et al. (2023)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/jbl.12336) | Short-run operating gains with a less favorable long-run valuation relation | A uniformly positive long-run stock effect |
| [TenderAlpha (2026)](https://www.tenderalpha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/White-Paper-Defense-Contract-Announcements-TenderAlpha-HKU-1.pdf) | Timestamped global-defense event study; relative-to-cap size shows a narrow 3-day relation | Significant absolute-dollar top-tail long-only post-publication effect; proprietary feed, non-peer-reviewed |

Mechanisms remain distinct: announcement reaction (A), post-publication drift (B), operating investment/backlog (C), and expected-return/risk-premium relation (D). The local candidate needs B or a separately proved tradeable D. A and C cannot be treated as B. Anticipation from solicitations, down-selects, incumbency and option cycles requires event-level classification.
