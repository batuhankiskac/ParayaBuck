# Concentration feasibility

**Top six: unproven. Top one: unproven and particularly fragile.** Retrieved 2026-09-22.

## Academic expression

[Cusatis et al. (1993)](https://longrunplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/restructuring-through-spinoffs.pdf) report an average over **all 146** pure children; their market-value-weighted calculation is for **parent plus child**, not a child-only liquid portfolio. [McConnell et al. (2001)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf) use all 96 children for event-level and calendar-time tests; observed child results change with benchmark and horizon. [McConnell and Ovtchinnikov (2004)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228289308_Predictability_of_Long-Term_Spin-Off_Returns) use 311 children, and [McConnell, Sibley and Xu (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf) use all 153. The last paper's implementable-style 22-month portfolio held **10–33 children** (26 at December 2006 inception), buying every qualifying spin rather than selecting a best six.

These studies do not report a child-return quintile or decile sorted on an ex-ante conviction variable with monotonic outperformance and liquid membership. Risk-control benchmark quintiles are **not** an investable rank of spin children. A focus-increasing cohort in [Desai and Jain (1999)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X9900032X) has economic rationale, but its publisher-accessible result is a binary cohort comparison, not within-cohort ranking; the table needed for a top-six transfer was not accessible. The [2023 Copenhagen master's thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/98732261/1642425_MastersThesis_Final.pdf) finds apparent gains mainly in 11 large-cap children, but within-size child abnormal results are not statistically significant, and the source mixes U.S. and Canadian events while using CapitalIQ. It cannot validate a U.S. large/liquid six-name rule.

## Top six

Six simultaneous positions would often omit most of the academic portfolio. In the 2015 full-text portfolio, six would represent 18%–60% of the 10–33 concurrent children, with no published ex-ante way to know which omitted names contain the upside. The same paper states its mean child advantage is materially right-skewed. One exceptional omitted winner could dominate the difference. Thus a six-name cap is an untested extrapolation, even if structural event supply reaches six.

## Top one

A single child inherits all firm-specific restructuring, litigation, financing, trading, and takeover dispersion. Neither the original matched-firm averages nor the later all-child portfolio estimates the performance of the ex-ante best individual child. The 2015 right skew makes top-one selection especially dependent on an externally validated rank; none is shown. A single satellite slot also risks being tied up for the reported long horizon.

**Concentration gate conclusion:** published evidence supports diversified ownership of eligible children far better than either required concentrated expression. Structural counts alone cannot turn an all-child average into top-six evidence.
