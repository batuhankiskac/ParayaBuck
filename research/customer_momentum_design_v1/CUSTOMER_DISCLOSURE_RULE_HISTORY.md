# Effective-dated disclosure rule history

| Filing date regime | Accounting major-customer rule | Customer name under GAAP | SEC Item 101 for ordinary registrants | Smaller reporting company | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016–2020-11-08 | ASC 280 successor to SFAS 131 para. 39: at >=10% of entity revenue, disclose the concentration, amount from each customer, and segment(s) | **Not required** | Historical 101(c)(1)(vii): disclose material customer dependence; name a >=10% revenue customer **when its loss would materially adversely affect** the registrant | Historical 101(h)(4)(vi) did not impose the same naming requirement | [FASB SFAS 131 para. 39](https://storage.fasb.org/fas131.pdf), [SEC 2016 disclosure proposal, p. 96](https://www.sec.gov/rules/proposed/2016/33-10110.pdf) |
| 2020-11-09–canonical 2026-07-30 endpoint | Same ASC 280 threshold/amount/segment requirement | **Not required** | Amended Item 101(c) uses material, principles-based description of dependence on customers, including government customers; old numerical naming provision removed | Amended Item 101(h) remains separate and less detailed | [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2020/33-10825.pdf), [effective-date FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/compliance-disclosure-interpretations/transitional-faqs-regarding-amended-regulation-s-k-items-101-103-105) |

SFAS 131 was effective for fiscal years beginning after 1997-12-15. Paragraph 39 expressly permits withholding the major customer's identity. It also treats each government as one customer. Thus Cohen–Frazzini's statement that SFAS 131 required the *identity* of every >10% customer is not the rule's text.

The SEC's 2016 explanation explicitly distinguished GAAP from Item 101(c)(1)(vii) and noted that the smaller-reporting-company provision did not require naming. In [2019 SEC correspondence](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/844143/000155837019006411/filename1.htm), staff challenged a registrant that reported a >10% largest customer without its name; this illustrates conditional enforcement, not universal naming. The 2020 amended Item 101(c)(1)(i) names customer dependence as a material topic but contains no old 10% naming rule. The switch is effective on **filing date**, not fiscal-year label. Forms 10-K and registration statements can invoke Item 101; 10-Q financial notes remain relevant under accounting/interim requirements, but quarterly edges are not automatically part of the paper's annual Compustat signal. 20-F/IFRS issuers are out of the proposed U.S. base.

This creates a plausible source-regime break. Its size has **not** been measured; no before/after coverage inference is made from law alone.
